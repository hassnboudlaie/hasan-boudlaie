# Reflective Leader alignment audit

Source: Armin Trost (2025), *The Reflective Leader: A Guide to Context-Driven Leadership and Building Trust*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-662-70860-6. The user-supplied English PDF has 176 PDF pages, with printed front matter and printed main text through index page 170. The complete text, references, appendix and index were read. Key tables on printed pages 78, 124 and 151 were additionally rendered and visually inspected. The PDF and extracted text are not published with this website.

All page references below and in the application use printed book pages, not the PDF viewer's ordinal. This is an independently worded applied learning course, not a translation or reproduction of the book. Saba Naft cases are original fictional applications, not accounts of company events.

## Reading coverage and interpretation

- Preface and ch. 1, pp. 1–3: conscious role definition and diversity, not a universal ideal; an everyday request for approval can elicit Boss, Coach, Partner or Enabler behavior.
- Ch. 2, pp. 5–14: diffuse dissatisfaction may involve unclarified expectations; digitalization/agility/top-management changes must be translated to daily behavior. The ten daily leadership challenges are explicitly a practical collection, not an empirically validated taxonomy.
- Ch. 3, pp. 15–26: traits, behaviors, contingency, LMX and path-goal theories are critically examined. The book challenges static, universal, leader-only prescriptions. The previous course's Managerial Grid must no longer serve as its main model or an assessment of the book's roles.
- Ch. 4, pp. 27–36: intuitive versus conscious action, trust and communication's relationship level. Experienced intuition is not invariably bad. A consciously explained role differs from an unconsidered reaction.
- Ch. 5, pp. 37–43: context → idea of leadership → leader–employee relationship, supported by compatible tools and continuous reflection. Ten hypotheses underpin the approach. No role is inherently superior. Leadership outcomes remain the manager's responsibility in all roles.
- Ch. 6, pp. 45–67: four nested context levels and 18 dimensions. Consider present/future with an explicitly specified future horizon. The context exercise is subjective reflection, not a psychometric diagnosis. One manager can operate in several different contexts. Skepticism can be constructive; cohesion can create groupthink. Distinguish structural time pressure from an isolated deadline. Key-function value and high-risk loss must be considered without assuming that either is a good/bad rating.
- Ch. 7, pp. 69–83: Boss owns decisions and externally directs; Coach expects self-regulation and thought; Partner shares substantive decisions and responsibility; Enabler builds capability and structural conditions. Boss/Coach are demanding; Boss/Partner imply proximity; Partner/Enabler invest. Table 7.1 is explicitly heuristic, not a validated factor model. Toxic counterparts are Tyrant, perpetual Therapist, Best Friend and overprotective Mother. Use neutral behavior descriptions in Persian while identifying the source terminology. A leader cannot delegate ultimate responsibility for team results. Avoid portraying Coach as a therapist or Enabler as rescuing.
- Ch. 8, pp. 85–105: an appropriate role mix is the leader's responsibility; distinguish stable context/primary roles from exceptional situation/secondary roles. There is deliberately no deterministic context-to-role formula. Personality and capability matter, but the book does not establish empirical role–Big Five or role–competency mappings. Use reflection, not invented personality tests. The book's role-mix exercise distributes **ten points**, with the manager's idea and their **assumed** employee expectations kept distinct. Actual employee input is not the manager's assumption.
- Ch. 9.1–9.4, pp. 107–119: role definition versus role attribution; mismatches can cause frustration even when intentions are positive. Role clarity is conveyed through consistent action, not declarations alone. Discuss gendered role expectations as bias, not as prescriptions or natural traits for every woman/man. Case practice should compare at least two reasonable alternatives; no straw-man choices.
- Ch. 9.5–9.7, pp. 119–126: clarify role expectations with the manager's own manager. No unsupported matrix predicts which hierarchy role combinations work. Reflection under stress distinguishes justified role changes from habitual dominant reactions. Four feedback perspectives: manager's idea, manager's self-observed behavior, employees' expectations and employees' experienced behavior. Results belong to learning and self-regulation, not centralized ranking. This website does not recreate or claim validation for Trost's unpublished/proprietary Leadership Role Inventory or Effectory product.
- Ch. 10, pp. 127–142: start with **benefit → context → tool → design**, not the tool. Eight benefit expectations, including clarifying the leadership relationship. Traditional appraisal is usually compatible with Boss, not automatically with the other roles. Differentiate feedback from praise, formal judgment and instruction. Feedback ownership and active seeking matter. In the book’s specific role typology, a Coach does not give feedback; a Partner can exchange it and an Enabler may provide it (p. 138). This is not generalized to all coaching schools. Compare standards, goal setting and OKRs without imposing one tool on every task. Address ability, motivation and permission when performance falls short; agree support and review. Show alternatives as design examples, not instructions to bypass company rules, compensation authority or statutory processes. Toolbox/navigation support differentiated use.
- Ch. 11, pp. 143–147: diversity of leadership, relational/systemic view, ongoing personal responsibility; no universal external benchmark determines a good leader.
- Appendix, pp. 149–160: context extremes, role overview, 15 leadership situations, questions for reflection/coaching. All 15 situation themes are represented by original Saba Naft applications. Four role options are plausible alternatives, not a normative answer key.
- References and index, pp. 161–170: inspected for scope, naming and page traceability.
